# Research notes: Larkspur Dental Studio, 12 South, Nashville (concept homepage)

## What I studied
- Studio EightyEight "20 best dental websites of 2025" and Lasso MD's top 20 list. I looked closely at Arbor, Boulder Smiles, Santa Rosa Dental Suite, Summit Dental Studio and Sweet Magnolia.
- Live homepages I fetched: Tend (hellotend.com), Boulder Smiles and Santa Rosa Dental Suite.
- Conversion articles: RevenueWell (6 ways to convert new patients), Lasso MD, plus 2026 CRO roundups from search results.
- Offer benchmarks: specials for uninsured new patients covering exam, X-rays and cleaning run $69 to $129 and say "in the absence of periodontal disease". In-house membership plans run $23 to $66 a month for two cleanings, exams and X-rays plus 10 to 20% off treatment.
- Tennessee Board of Dentistry advertising rule, Tenn. Comp. R. & Regs. 0460-02-.10 (fetched from Cornell LII), and the ADA Code, which Tennessee adopts by reference in 0460-01-.13.

## What the best homepages do
- Hero: one line about the experience, not the dentistry. One booking CTA plus the phone number, with a rating near the CTA.
- Order: hero and trust, services, doctor and team, comfort or technology, reviews, insurance and membership, hours and location, FAQ.
- Photography: natural light, real rooms, candid moments between team and patient. The calm sites never show procedures up close.

## Tennessee rules and how the page follows them
- A general dentist who advertises specialty-type services must say "the services are being performed or provided by a general dentist", as conspicuously as the services. The page says this beside the services heading, in the same serif as the service names, and again in the footer. The page never uses "specialist" or "specializing".
- Testimonials must be reasonably verifiable. The page notes "from verified patients, shared with permission. Individual experiences and results vary".
- A discounted fee must state the usual fee, name any extra fees and be honoured for at least 30 days. The page shows "our usual fee is $380", says further treatment is quoted separately, and runs the offer to Dec 31, 2026, about 60 days away.
- No superiority claims and no fear appeals. Copy reassures but never threatens, and cosmetic and aligner copy says results vary. The membership is labelled "not insurance".

## Market and decisions
- 12 South is a walkable, design-conscious Nashville neighbourhood full of young professionals and families, many new to the city. That supports a boutique studio tone, late Tuesday and Thursday hours, and named nearby areas: Belmont, Green Hills, Sylvan Park, The Gulch, Berry Hill, East Nashville and Brentwood.
- Insurance copy names the major PPOs and says plainly that the practice does not take TennCare or HMO plans. That is a realistic stance for a fee-for-service boutique.
- The signature section is a minute-by-minute first-visit timeline, because surprises are what anxious patients fear most. The pricing section is a real comparison table: membership against PPO insurance against no plan.
- Following update 2: only three eyebrow labels, no icon card grids or stats boxes, and no map illustration. A different structure for every section. One cool grade across every photo. "Book an appointment" appears down the page and in a sticky mobile bar.
- Following update 3: the hero now shows a hygienist with a smiling patient in a blue dental chair under the overhead light, so the industry reads in two seconds, and the photo takes 56% of the desktop width and over half of the first phone screen. The page has 34 photos: a thumbnail for every service, a photo for every step of the first visit, an "Inside the studio" gallery and photos in the offer and pricing sections, mostly from one Pexels shoot so the rooms and hygienist match.
