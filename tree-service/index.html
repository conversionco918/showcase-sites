What we do

From one bad limb
to a whole lot.

Most calls are for one of seven things. Every one is quoted by an arborist, done by our own crew, and finished with the yard raked and blown.

  • Tree removal

    Dead, leaning, split or just in the wrong place. We take trees down in sections, lower every piece on a rope instead of dropping it, and lay ground mats so the lawn survives the day.

    Removals from $1,100 · haul-off included

  • Trimming & pruning

    Pruned to ANSI A300 standards by climbers who know where to cut and where to stop. Roof clearance, thinning for wind, deadwood out before it falls on the car. We don't top trees.

    Most pruning $550 to $1,800

  • Stump grinding

    Ground 8 to 12 inches below grade so you can replant or lay sod. Grindings go back in the hole or on the truck, your call. Topsoil and seed if you want it.

    $175 first stump · $85 each after that

  • Storm work

    Limbs on the roof, a pine across the driveway, a split hanging over the bedroom. A person answers the storm line at any hour. We make it safe first and clean up properly after.

    Standard rates · no storm surcharge

  • Plant health care

    Deep-root fertilization, soil decompaction after construction, borer and disease treatment, and cabling for split trunks. Our technicians hold Georgia pesticide applicator licenses, and an arborist checks the results.

    Visits from $180 · written plan

  • Crane-assisted removal

    Most intown lots have no drop zone. When a tree leans over the house, our own 40-ton crane lifts the pieces out over the roof. Fewer climbs, a shorter day and much less weight on the lawn.

    From $4,500 · usually one day

  • Lot clearing

    Privet, kudzu, volunteer pines and overgrown fence lines on bigger lots. Forestry mulching leaves the ground covered, with no burn piles and no ruts.

    Priced per acre · after a site walk

Your yard is
our job site.

Tree work is one of the most dangerous trades there is. A cheap quote from an uninsured crew can turn into your problem the moment someone gets hurt on your property.

  • ANSI Z133 on every site

    Helmets, eye and ear protection, chainsaw chaps, and a tailgate safety talk before the first saw starts. Every day, every crew.

  • Insurance you can see first

    $2M general liability and workers' comp for all 38 employees. Your certificate of insurance arrives before our trucks do.

  • No spikes in trees we're keeping

    Climbing spikes wound living wood, so we only use them on removals. Pruning climbs are done on rope.

  • Power lines left to the power company

    If a line runs through the canopy, we coordinate with the utility before anyone leaves the ground.

How much does tree removal cost in Atlanta?

Most of our residential removals land between $1,100 and $4,200. A small tree in an open backyard can be under $800. A big oak over the house that needs the crane usually starts around $4,500.

Access, what is underneath, and whether the city needs a permit all move the number. That's why every estimate is free and done on site.

Do I need a permit to take a tree down?

Inside the City of Atlanta, usually yes. Most trees 6 inches in diameter or larger need a permit under the city's tree protection ordinance, including dead and hazardous ones. Permits for healthy trees are posted at the property for a public notice period before work can start, and a replacement fee may apply.

Decatur, Brookhaven, Sandy Springs and Dunwoody each have their own rules. We check your address, handle the paperwork and give you the timeline in writing.

Are you licensed and insured?

Georgia doesn't license tree removal at the state level, which is exactly why insurance and certification matter. We carry $2M in general liability and workers' comp for every employee and email your certificate before the job. Four of us hold ISA certification, and our plant health technicians are licensed pesticide applicators in Georgia.

When is the best time to prune?

For most of Atlanta's big oaks, late fall through winter. The leaves are off, the structure is easy to read and the tree handles it well. Dead limbs, and anything hanging over the house, can come out any time of year.

Will my homeowners insurance pay for storm damage?

Often, when a tree falls on a covered structure like the house, garage or fence. It depends on your policy. We document every storm job with photos, measurements and an itemized invoice your adjuster can use.

What happens to the wood?

We haul it all away unless you'd like to keep some. Plenty of people keep the wood cut into firewood rounds, and a load of fresh chips for your beds is free.

How soon can you come out?

Walkthroughs are usually within three business days, and most scheduled work happens within two weeks. Storm calls inside I-285 are seen the same day, usually within two to four hours.
