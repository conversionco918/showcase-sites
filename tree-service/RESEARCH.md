# Research notes: Post Oak Tree Co. (Atlanta tree service concept)

**What I studied.** Davey Tree and Monster Tree Service (national), the regional sites in the Zarla, Jobber and Colorlib round-ups (SavATree, Austin Tree Specialists, TreeCareLA, Capitol Tree Care, Aaron's Tree Service), and conversion guides from Kelly WM, ResultCalls and The Nine.

**Section order the best sites share.** Hero with a free-estimate CTA, then credentials, services, storm or emergency service, proof (gallery and reviews), the estimate form, FAQ, then the footer with hours. Credentials and insurance always show up in the first scroll. They never sit only in the footer.

**Trust signals they lead with.** ISA Certified Arborists, "fully insured" (the better sites give a dollar figure and mention workers' comp), years in business, review rating and count, and real crew photos. Decision: a quiet credential line under the hero CTA (4 arborists, $2M plus workers' comp, 4.9 from 410+), then an owner's note with the numbers set as a ruled list. No badge row.

**CTA wording.** "Get a Free Estimate" beats "Contact Us" because it says what happens next. Emergency visitors want a phone number, not a form. Decision: one primary action, "Get a free estimate", used word for word 9 times (header, hero, after services, after pricing, quote band, form, footer, mobile menu, sticky bar). The 24/7 storm line gets its own strip and section.

**Standard offer.** A free on-site estimate with a written price, often with a seasonal discount. Decision: free walkthrough by a certified arborist, fixed written price within 24 hours, and 10% off dormant-season pruning booked by Feb 28 (it is November, so the winter offer fits).

**Forms.** Four or five fields, a photo upload, and a line saying what happens next. Decision: name, phone, neighborhood, service, an optional note, a styled photo drop zone and call or text preference. The success message lists the next three steps.

**Photography.** Real crews in PPE, climbers on rope, before and after shots, big healthy trees. Decision: 17 Pexels photos with one shared earthy grade. The climber is the hero. Every section uses a real photo, with no drawn maps or illustrations.

**Atlanta market.** Atlanta calls itself the "city in a forest" and has one of the densest urban canopies of any big US city. The common trees are water and white oaks, tulip poplars and loblolly pines. Summer thunderstorms bring down big trees often, and Tropical Storm Irma did serious tree damage across the metro in 2017. I used the intown neighborhoods (Virginia-Highland, Druid Hills, Ansley Park, Inman Park) and the northern suburbs (Brookhaven, Sandy Springs, Dunwoody, Roswell, Marietta), and put the yard in Chamblee.

**Local rules.** The City of Atlanta's tree protection ordinance requires a permit to remove most trees about 6 inches in diameter or larger on private property, including dead and hazardous ones. Permits for healthy trees get posted for a public notice period, and replacement fees can apply. Nearby cities set their own rules. Georgia has no state license for tree removal, so ISA certification and insurance are the credentials homeowners should check. Pesticide work needs a Georgia applicator licence. Decision: a permit-handling line in the pricing table, a permit callout in the service area section and a permit FAQ. I wrote the permit rule in general terms because I could not load the city code page to confirm the exact threshold and posting days.

**Advertising care.** No ISA or other association logos (members only, and this business is fictional), no third-party review badges, prices given as ranges with "from", and no promises we can't verify, like "lowest price".

**Name check.** I rejected "Highline Tree" and "Ironwood Tree Care" (several real US companies each), "Timberline" (a common brand and a Zarla template name) and "Cross Timbers" (a real arborist in OKC). Searches found no company called "Post Oak Tree Co." in Oklahoma or nationally. Search engines blocked my Georgia-specific re-check, so it is worth one more look before anything goes public.
