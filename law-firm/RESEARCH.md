# Research notes: Calder & Thorne Injury Law (Houston)

What we studied
- Award and agency roundups of personal injury sites (Juris Digital, Dnovo Group, GrowLaw, OnTheMap lists) and real homepages for Malman Law, Pitta & Baione, Hendler Flores and West Coast Trial Lawyers.
- Conversion articles for injury firms (Plaintiff Authority's 12 conversion wins, Nifty Marketing on PI CTAs, Walker Advertising on 2026 trends).
- Texas Disciplinary Rules of Professional Conduct Part VII (Rules 7.01 and 7.02 on legalethicstexas.com), plus the ABA Model Rule 7.1 comments. An earlier Tulsa draft used the Oklahoma rules; the site moved to Houston and was rewritten for Texas.

What the best homepages do, and what we took from it
- Order: hero with phone and one primary action, proof (results, ratings), practice areas, how a case works, attorneys, reviews, FAQ, form, office. We kept that order but gave every section a different structure (ledger, ruled two-column list, photo split, letter, table, long quote, fee table) so the page does not read as a template.
- CTA wording: "Free case review" beats "consultation" (sounds like an evaluation, not a sales call). Phone is a tap-to-call link everywhere, plus a sticky call bar on phones.
- Trust signals lead with specific recoveries and named attorneys with real-looking credentials, not badges. We show no Google, Avvo or Super Lawyers marks; the rating is text only.
- Best sites write results as short case stories (what happened, where, what it was) instead of bare numbers. We added county, year and lead counsel to each.
- Spanish matters in Houston: Spanish-speaking partner, bilingual intake, a Spanish review with translation, and a language choice in the form.
- Photography: real-looking attorneys in their own office beat handshake stock. We used one consistent law-office session plus night shots of Houston freeways and skyline, all given the same cool grade.

Texas advertising rules applied
- Rule 7.02(a): the footer names a responsible attorney (Margaret Calder) and the primary practice location (Houston).
- Rule 7.02(c): every "no fee unless we recover" line says what happens to expenses. This firm advances them, is repaid from a recovery and waives them if there is no recovery; the fees section shows the 33 1/3% and 40% tiers and a worked example.
- Rule 7.01(a) and comments: results carry a "past results do not guarantee a similar outcome" note and say amounts are gross. Each result names the attorney who led it (substantial role). The one jury verdict says it was paid in full (Rule 7.01(g) requires the amount actually received if a verdict was reduced).
- No "specialist", "expert", "best" or comparisons with other firms. Footer notes the attorneys are not certified by the Texas Board of Legal Specialization (Rule 7.02(b) limits certification claims).
- Form note: submitting does not create an attorney-client relationship. Reviews carry a "no promise of any particular result" line.
- Before a real launch: confirm whether the State Bar of Texas Advertising Review Committee filing rules apply to the site, and add a street address if the firm wants it.

Texas law facts used in copy: two-year limitations period for injury and wrongful death; six-month notice for most government claims; proportionate responsibility with a 51% bar; PIP included unless rejected in writing; employers can opt out of workers' comp (nonsubscribers); no dog bite statute.

Name check: "Calder & Thorne" and the attorney names (Margaret Calder, Elliot Thorne, Simone Barlow) turned up no Texas or national law firm or lawyer in our searches. "Hale" (Tulsa injury firm), "Kane"/"Cain" (Tulsa injury firms) and "Rhodes" (Tulsa defense firm) were rejected during the first pass.
