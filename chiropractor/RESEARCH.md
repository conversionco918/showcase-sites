# Research notes: chiropractic homepage (Denver)

What I studied
- Roundups of top chiropractic sites (WebCitz's expert audit of 14 sites, Chirobasix's 20 real examples) plus the live homepages of Eagle Trace Spine & Sport (MN), Complete Chiropractic (Durham NC) and The Joint Chiropractic (national).
- Conversion write-ups: Kelly WM "What actually converts in 2026", ChiropractorSEOService on high-converting landing pages, Clinic Publisher on CTAs.
- Compliance: FTC health-claim guidance summaries (Illinois Chiropractic Society, Chiropractic Economics) and state board advertising rules (the Oklahoma and Texas rules came up in my research; I applied the same caution for Colorado).

What the best homepages share
- The new patient offer leads, and it repeats in the top bar, the hero and again near the booking form. A typical price is $47 to $79 for consult, exam, X-rays if needed and first adjustment, shown against a "regularly $185 to $350" anchor.
- The usual order is hero with offer, trust, conditions, approach, doctor, reviews, insurance, FAQ, then the booking CTA. This page repeats "Book your first visit" 9 times and adds a sticky mobile bar with Book and Call, which matches ConversionCo client builds.
- They lead with trust: a plain review count, years in the neighbourhood, real doctor and room photos, and "we verify your benefits before your first visit".
- The CTA copy is concrete: "Book your first visit", "Book my $47 visit". Friction reducers sit next to it: no referral needed, benefits checked, no packages.

Denver market and decisions
- Denver patients are active: trail runners, cyclists, skiers and climbers, plus desk workers in RiNo and LoDo. The conditions list adds "Running and riding injuries" and "Ski and climbing strains". The goal photos are mountain hikes, and the review stories mention a fourteener, a powder day and a climbing gym.
- I put the clinic in Lower Highlands (LoHi), near the Platte River Trail and a walk from Union Station. It serves Highland, Sloan's Lake, Berkeley, Sunnyside, Wash Park, Cherry Creek, Arvada and Wheat Ridge. Denver pricing runs higher than Tulsa, so the offer is $69 (usually $195) and follow-ups are $65.
- Colorado lets patients see a chiropractor directly, with no referral needed. Auto care mentions MedPay because Colorado is an at-fault state with optional MedPay. The licence line names the Colorado State Board of Chiropractic Examiners. Dr. Hartwell's credentials stick to the DC degree, her school and the state licence, with no specialist or certification claims.
- Compliance: no cure language. "Results vary" sits beside the outcome stories and the reviews. The offer carries a Medicare/federal plan exclusion. There are no insurer or review-platform logos.
- Name: Riverline Chiropractic. My earlier national searches found no business by that name anywhere, and Alignwell and Northpoint were already taken by real clinics. My search budget ran out before I could run a Colorado-only check, so that re-check was limited (see the report).

Design decisions (brief update 2)
- Palette: white and cool grey (#F2F3F5) bases, charcoal (#1E1F22) bands, golden yellow (#F2B33D) accent with dark text on it. It reads as a bright performance clinic and doesn't overlap the other four sites.
- I cut back on AI tells. There are only 3 eyebrows. Every section has its own structure: a split hero, a plain intro paragraph with a facts table, a photo list, a full-bleed band, offset goal photos, alternating photo rows, a dark timeline, a bio with a credentials list, one long testimonial plus a two-column review list, a yellow offer strip, a real pricing table, the FAQ and booking. There are no stat boxes. The numbers sit inline in a sentence.
- I replaced the drawn map with a photo of the clinic room, and every image slot is now a photograph (14 in total).
